Threads as one conversation

docs / specs / threads.spec.html

Every surface Spec Chat draws threads on reads them as one conversation, the way a chat app does: each message a bubble, threads in the order they began, each thread's replies folded under the message they answer, and a tap on any message to read that thread alone. This spec owns how threads read on a spec page, a mockup page (mockups), and a host page through the comment layer (comment layer). What a thread holds and how copies merge stay in Shared review state; when a thread is resolved or reopened stays in thread lifecycle.

User stories

As a human reviewer, I read every thread on a page as one conversation in time order, my messages on one side and the agent's on the other, so a review reads like a chat, not a stack of separate threads.

As a human reviewer, each thread's replies fold under the message they answer and open in place, and a tap on a message shows that thread alone with the rest dimmed, so each thread keeps its own context inside the one conversation.

As a human reviewer, a thread's first message names the spot it is about, a block, a quote, a part of a mockup at a step, or a moment of a video, and a tap on that name takes me there.

As a human reviewer, I see each thread's state as a receipt under my newest message, Draft, Sent, Seen, or Resolved, and a dot on every thread whose last word is the agent's, so I know what waits on me.

As a human reviewer, the agent's open questions on a page read as its messages at the end of the conversation, with their answer buttons, so I answer each where I read it and my answer stays with its question.

As a human reviewer, I comment on a moment of a video a page shows, with a box on the frame when I mean one part of it, and a tap on that comment's moment plays the video back to it with the box drawn.

As a human reviewer, each message shows the stage its lane was at when it was written, such as Spec review or Code review, so I read a thread's history in the lane's terms.

As the owning agent, my scan gives each comment the spot it is about, its step or moment included, gives a question its newest answer with each earlier one marked superseded, and lists a batch's threads in the order the conversation shows them.

Acceptance criteria

Given a Spec Chat contributor's change to any feature that shows threads, and a reviewer, When the reviewer opens those threads on any surface Spec Chat draws, Then they read as one conversation as one conversation states, threads in the order they began with their replies folded under them, and no surface shows them as separate thread cards or a separate thread list.

Given a human reviewer on a desktop on a spec page holding three threads begun at different times on different blocks, When the reviewer opens the review panel, Then the panel shows the three threads' first messages as bubbles in the order they were written, the newest at the bottom, each with a chip naming its block.

Modular boundaries

The browser runtime owns the conversation on every page it reviews: the stream, bubbles, chips, folded replies, the focused thread, receipts, waiting dots, question bubbles, and video moments and boxes as comment targets. Caller-facing seam: the page, the review panel, the comment layer's item box, and the comment events it saves through the service's routes. Dependency direction: external-provider; the runtime depends on the service's routes and the shared fold, never the reverse. Observable scope: the review panel, the item box, pins, the video rail, and the comment events' spot fields.

Shared review state owns what a comment carries, its step, moment, and stage included, and the fold every reader shares. Caller-facing seam: the event fields (document model). Dependency direction: self-contained; the conversation is a reading of the existing fold and adds no event and no status. Observable scope: spool events.

The review skill owns the owner's side: the scan's rows with each comment's spot, a question's newest answer and the answers it supersedes, and a batch in stream order. Caller-facing seam: the scan's rows. Dependency direction: self-contained on disk; it reads the fold every reader shares and needs no running service. Observable scope: scan output.

A host page, such as a peer's evidence page, owns which items and videos it shows; the comment layer draws their conversation. Caller-facing seam: the anchor attributes (hosts and anchors), and a video inside an anchored item. Dependency direction: external-provider; a host depends on the layer, and Spec Chat names no host. Observable scope: none in Spec Chat beyond the layer's controls.

One conversation

A conversation holds the threads of one context: on a spec page or a mockup page, every thread of its review; on a host page, the threads of the item whose comment control is open. The review panel of a spec page and of a mockup page is its conversation; the comment layer's item box is its item's; and the comment UI page a shell frames shows the conversation of the page the shell relayed last, by the same rule (comment UI page).

Threads read in the order their first messages were written, the newest at the bottom, and the conversation opens scrolled to its bottom. A thread stays where it began when a reply is added to it; its replies line and its dot show the new word.

Every message is a bubble: this browser's own messages at the right, on the action color; the agent's and other reviewers' at the left, on the soft surface; each run of bubbles headed by its author's fruit name, this browser's included, and the agent's by Agent (review state). A draft shows on the soft action color, outlined, until it is handed off; a message an author edited shows its effective text, marked Edited.

The panel's head reads Conversation and counts its threads and its open questions.

A comment saved from comment mode starts a new thread at the bottom; a reply saved in a focused thread joins its thread.

Replies fold under what they answer

Under a thread's first message, its replies fold into one line: the reply count, then the newest reply's author and first words. Pressing that line opens the replies in place under the first message, in the order written, and pressing it again folds them; on a phone as on a desktop.

Opening one thread's replies changes no other thread, so each thread keeps its own context while the conversation still reads top to bottom.

A thread with no reply shows no replies line.

The focused thread

Pressing any message of a thread, or its pin on the page, opens that thread focused: its messages alone, in order, with the reply field and Resolve under them, over the rest of the review panel or item box dimmed, its header and hand-off bar included. A focused question keeps the hand-off bar above the dim, so Next TBD steps on (Next TBD).

On a desktop the focused thread fills the review panel, which opens if it was closed; Back returns to the conversation where it was.

On a phone it is a sheet over the lower part of the screen, under its top half, so the spot stays in view above it, with the page dimmed around the spot; the bottom toolbar's one hand-off control stays under the sheet (mobile review).

A reply saved there joins the thread, and the conversation shows it in the thread's replies line once the focused thread closes.

A resolved thread opens focused with its whole history, and offers Reply and reopen when its latest message is the agent's (thread lifecycle).

A thread whose newest message is the agent's and that is not resolved waits on the reviewer: a dot on its first message's bubble and on its pin, until a reviewer replies or resolves it.

Questions in the conversation

Each open TBD question of the page (answer buttons) reads as the agent's bubble: its question, its answer buttons, a Reply for an answer in words, and a chip naming its block, or on a mockup page its part and step (mockup questions).

Unanswered questions close the conversation, in page order; a question with an answer sits where its first answer was written.

Pressing an answer button in a question's bubble saves the same answer comment as pressing that button on the page: the same block, the same button, the same chosen mark on both, and no composer, panel, or full-viewport sheet opens. The answer shows as the reviewer's reply under the question.

A question's answer is its newest answer comment, by a button or in words; each earlier one is superseded by it. The scan reports the newest as the question's answer and each earlier one as superseded, so a changed mind needs no retraction and a short answer is never read out of its question.

A moment of a video

Every video a reviewed page shows, on a spec page, a mockup page, or inside an anchored item of a host page, is a comment target at a moment. In comment mode, clicking the video marks the moment it shows, pausing it first if it plays; dragging across it first draws a box on the frame, and the comment marks the moment with that box. On a host page, a comment saved on an anchored item holding a paused video marks the moment it shows.

The comment carries its moment, the video's time in seconds, and its box, as fractions of the frame's width and height, beside its anchor (review state).

Under each video with threads, a rail as wide as the video marks each thread at its moment; pressing a mark seeks there, pauses, draws its box, and opens its thread focused. While the video is paused at a thread's moment, its box shows on the frame.

A video Spec Chat shows is one the page already holds, or a mockup's round video its recorder stores (round video); Spec Chat records and cuts no video.

The stage a message was written in

Every message carries the stage its lane was at when it was written, in the words the box gives, such as Shaping round 2, Spec review, Code review, QA, Merge ask, or Done, shown as a small tag on its bubble beside its author.

The stage is stamped once, when the message is written, and never changes: an edit keeps its message's stage, and a later stage shows only on later messages.

A message with no lane carries none, and so does one written while the box names no stage for its lane; its bubble shows no tag. An open question is the page's text, not a message, so its bubble shows no stage; the answers to it carry theirs.

A message's lane is the lane its thing names, which a host gives with its anchor (anchor lane), so a thread about a lane's delivery kept in another scope still has that lane; else its review's or scope's slug when that slug holds a lane's specs; else none. A comment records the lane it took, and a reply, the agent's included, has its thread's lane, so a reply on a thread about another lane's work carries that lane's stage. The stage stamped is its lane's stage when written, which the review service tells each page for each lane its threads have, from the review facts it asks (review facts); the agent's reply takes it from its session's service the same way (review state). Spec Chat names no workflow and reads the words as given, as it reads a kind.

A picture of a boxed spot

A comment on a box may carry a picture its page made of the boxed spot as the human saw it, the box drawn on it, such as a page that draws on a canvas and has no text to quote: a PNG, JPEG, or WebP image of at most 1 MB.

The comment keeps it for as long as it keeps the comment (stores), and the focused thread shows it under the comment's bubble; the chip stays words and still leads to the spot as it now is (chip).

The owner's scan names the picture's file with the comment, so the agent can look at what the human saw.

Why this way

The conversation is a reading of the threads review state keeps: the same fold, statuses, and events, drawn as a chat. It adds no event, status, store, or second fold, so the agent's scan, the review index, and every page keep reading the same threads.

Threads stay where they began, with replies folded under them, rather than each reply joining the bottom of the conversation: a review has many threads about many spots, and a reply read away from its spot is the ambiguity the conversation exists to remove.

Chips name a spot in words, not a picture: a picture is a second copy that drifts from the page, and the chip's press shows the spot itself. A box's picture is no chip: it is what the human saw when they wrote, kept beside their words as evidence, never as the way to the spot.

Proof

Seam: the conversation's order, folding, receipts, and dots, derived from fixture spools by the runtime's fold; the scan's spot fields and superseded answers over fixture spools; the panel, focused thread, question bubbles, and video moments through fixture pages in a browser test, on a desktop and a phone, on a spec page and through the comment layer.

First failing test: a fixture spool of three threads begun at different times renders as three first-message bubbles in that order, each with its chip and its replies folded.
