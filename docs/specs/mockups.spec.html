Mockups beside the spec

docs / specs / mockups.spec.html

A mockup is one screen a spec describes, reviewed beside the spec at desktop and phone. A screen the project owns is its running prototype: the project's own frontend code on the lane branch, a route of its app or a harness page of its shipped components, that Spec Chat frames for review at the lane's own prototype origin and the build keeps; only chrome the project does not own is drawn, as a page beside the spec. A screen's first round may show two or three directions side by side, for the reviewer to pick from with one press (directions). Shaping writes it (shaping), the review service serves it with its spec, and a reviewer comments on any element at any step, answers the questions pinned in place, hands off, and accepts it with the spec, as on the spec page. The spec stays canonical for behavior; the mockup is its visual reference. Review state stays in Shared review state, the service in Box review service and providers, and the review index in Review index.

User stories

As a human reviewer, I open a mockup of a screen the spec describes, see that screen at desktop and phone, comment on any part of either the way I comment on a spec, hand off with the one hand-off control, and read the owner's answer in that thread, on a desktop or a phone.

As a human reviewer, a mockup shows the screen as the product will ship it, in the app's own chrome, components, fonts, tokens, and words, with every control's states and the product's own motion, and each part it adds explained by the layout rather than by extra labels and descriptions, so I review the real screen and never send it back to be redone at production quality.

As a human reviewer, I review a screen as its running prototype, built on the lane branch from the product's shipped components, and comment on any element of it at any step, so what I accept is the code the build keeps.

As a human reviewer, each round I watch a short captioned video recorded from the prototype as it now runs, on a desktop or a phone, and comment on any moment of it.

As a human reviewer, a screen's first round shows its directions side by side on its mockup page, each with the one line saying what it gives up and the agent's pick with its reason; one toggle switches them all between desktop and phone, and one press makes another direction the pick, so I choose between real options in the round I review anyway, on a desktop or a phone.

As the lane that builds an accepted spec, I keep each screen's prototype components and finish them on their routes, so the build cannot drift from what review accepted, and QA compares the build with that prototype.

As a human reviewer, I play a screen's walkthrough, the steps it goes through with its real motion, jump to any step, or press through it as I would the product, on a desktop or a phone.

As a human reviewer, I comment on any part of a mockup at the step showing, and the thread names that part and step and takes me back to it.

As a human reviewer, the shaping agent's questions about a screen sit on its mockup at the part and step they are about; I go question by question, answer each with one press or in my own words where it is asked, and each answer stays with its question.

As a project whose screens a spec changes, I offer one prototype command that serves my app's routes and my harness pages at their root with my fixture data, so every screen I own is reviewed as my own code renders it, and my code carries nothing of Spec Chat's.

As a feature author, the structural validator fails a mockup I add or edit that neither names a prototype route nor draws its frames, and a drawn mockup that names no product whose chrome it draws, names no design source or one changed since the commit it was read at, uses a color, duration, or easing outside the design tokens, loads a font file it lacks, draws no focus or disabled state for its controls, or moves with no reduced-motion rule, naming the mockup and what is wrong.

As a human reviewer, when a change edits a mockup, I switch between the screen before the change and after it, against the base the spec page compares, so I see what changed without reading a description of it.

As a human reviewer reading a criterion or clause a mockup illustrates, I press its See mockup chip and land on the part of the screen that shows it.

As a human reviewer opening a spec page, I see at its top every screen the spec has, each by name, whether it is a running prototype or a drawn mockup, its viewports, and its open threads and questions, and open any of them to comment on it in one press, on a desktop or a phone.

As a human reviewer approving or commenting on each screen in turn, I go from a screen's mockup page to the next or previous screen in the order the Screens index lists them, with one press or an arrow key, on a desktop or a phone, without going back to the spec page.

As a human reviewer, Accept spec accepts the spec with its mockups, and is offered only once every thread on the spec and on its mockups is resolved, so one acceptance covers what a screen does and how it looks.

As the owning agent, a hand-off on a mockup wakes me as one on the spec does, my scan names the mockup, and I edit the mockup and reply as I do for a spec.

As a tool that reads accepted mockups, such as a QA check comparing a built screen with its mockup, I find a spec's mockups from the spec's path, each screen's prototype route or its desktop and phone frame, its walkthrough's steps, and the anchors each part and step illustrates, at the commit the spec was accepted at.

Acceptance criteria

Given a human reviewer on a phone on a mockup page whose desktop frame shows a button, When the reviewer taps Comment, then taps that button, then types a comment and saves it, then closes the review panel, Then the button shows a pin.

Given a human reviewer on a desktop on a mockup page with a draft comment, When the reviewer opens the review panel, then presses Hand off, Then the notice Handed off 1 comment, agent notified shows, and the comment's thread shows the receipt Sent.

Given a human reviewer on a desktop on a mockup page with no thread, When the reviewer adds a draft comment on a button, then opens the review panel, Then the screen shows exactly one Hand off button, the drafts note reads 1 draft, and no Accept spec, Next TBD, Jev, or evidence control shows.

Given a human reviewer on a phone on a mockup page with no thread, When the reviewer adds a draft comment on a button, then opens the review panel, Then the screen shows exactly one Hand off button, the drafts note reads 1 draft, and no Accept spec, Next TBD, Jev, or evidence control shows.

Given a human reviewer with a mockup holding a handed-off comment on a button, When the reviewer opens that mockup's page, then waits while the agent inserts a new element above that button, then reloads the page, then opens that comment's thread by its pin, Then the pin sits on the same button, and its thread shows no change notice.

Given a human reviewer on a mockup page whose mockup changed since the base its spec page compares, When the reviewer presses Before, Then the page shows both frames as the mockup was at that base, with no pins and no comment control, and Before shows as pressed.

Given a human reviewer on a mockup page whose mockup changed since the base its spec page compares and holds a thread, When the reviewer presses Before, then presses After, Then the page shows the current frames with the thread's pin, and After shows as pressed.

Modular boundaries

The shaping skill owns writing a spec's mockup set at production fidelity: which screens it holds, each screen's prototype route or drawing, a first round's directions, their walkthroughs, the questions it asks on them, and the anchors each part and step illustrates; the prototype's code is the product's, written on the lane branch outside the skill. Caller-facing seam: the mockup files in the set's folder and their markup. Dependency direction: self-contained; it reads the spec, the repo's design source, and its fixtures, and names no reader. Observable scope: the committed mockup files.

The review service owns serving a registered spec's mockup set under its row, keeping each mockup's spool beside it, telling a spec page and its mockup pages its set with each mockup's prototype route, how many directions it shows, and its open threads and questions, and waking the row's owner for a mockup's hand-off. Caller-facing seam: the spec's registry row, with no registration of its own, and the routes a spec page and a mockup page call. Dependency direction: self-contained; it reads the row's worktree, its spools, and local Git, and reaches owners only through the wake provider. Observable scope: served mockup pages, their spools and versions, and wake prompts.

The browser runtime owns the mockup page's review, its frames' fit, its directions with their Desktop and Phone toggle and Pick, Before and After, the step bar, the walkthrough's play, threads at a step, question marks, Next TBD, and Previous and Next on a mockup page, and the See mockup chips, the Screens index, and open mockup threads and questions on the spec page. Caller-facing seam: the mockup markup and the service's routes. Dependency direction: external-provider; the runtime depends on the service's routes, never the reverse. Observable scope: pins, question marks, the conversation, the one hand-off control, the directions, the top line with Previous and Next, the step bar, the frames, the chips, and the Screens index.

The review skill owns the owner's side of a mockup's review: the scan with each comment's step and each question's answer, edits to the mockup, replies, and the cursor, as for a spec. Caller-facing seam: the scan's rows, each naming the mockup it comes from. Dependency direction: self-contained on disk; it folds a mockup's spool as it folds a spec's and needs no running service. Observable scope: scan output, agent events, and the cursor.

The structural validator owns the mockup check of a changed spec's set, fidelity included. Caller-facing seam: the validator's exit status and the problems it names. Dependency direction: self-contained; it reads the spec, its set's files, the design source files they name, and local Git. Observable scope: validator output.

The prototype, product code on the lane branch, owns its routes, harness pages, components, data, and steps, and how it runs for the lane. Caller-facing seam: its routes at their root at the address its row names, served by the prototype command its repo names (seam); the mockup file's selectors and actions reach into it, and its code carries nothing of Spec Chat's. Dependency direction: external-provider; Spec Chat depends on the routes the mockup file names, the prototype depends on nothing of Spec Chat's, and Spec Chat names no product. Observable scope: none in Spec Chat beyond the routes its mockup pages frame.

A recorder owns recording each round's videos from the prototype, doing each step's actions in order, and storing them with their captions. Caller-facing seam: the mockup's videos folder and its file names. Dependency direction: external-provider; a recorder depends on the steps and the folder, and Spec Chat names no recorder. Observable scope: the video and caption files in the mockup's spool.

A reader of accepted mockups, such as a QA check that drives each step of a screen and compares the built screen with its accepted prototype, owns what it does with them (who owns what). Caller-facing seam: the set's folder beside the spec, the markup, and a caller's spec acceptance receipt naming the spec at a commit. Dependency direction: external-provider; a reader depends on this format, and Spec Chat names no reader. Observable scope: none in Spec Chat.

The mockup set

A mockup is one screen of the product as its spec intends it: the screen's running prototype, or a drawn page for chrome the project does not own (drawn chrome). The spec stays canonical for behavior: where a mockup and its spec disagree, the spec wins and the mockup is out of date.

A spec's mockup set is the folder mockups/<spec name>/ beside the spec, where <spec name> is the spec's file name without .spec.html. Each screen is one file in that folder, <NN>-<screen>.mockup.html: NN two digits that order the screens, <screen> its name in lowercase words joined by hyphens. Images, font files, and other files a mockup shows sit in the same folder, or in mockups/ itself when several sets show them.

Like its spec, a mockup shows the target state only, never a before or history; Git keeps the before, which Before shows. Its only alternatives are a first round's directions, which the round after removes (directions).

A drawn mockup is self-contained: it carries its own styles, written with the design source's tokens by their names and values (production fidelity); it loads the page runtime from the spec folder and nothing from outside the spec folder, so it renders the same from any server and at any commit. It carries no script of its own: its steps are drawn by its styles (walkthrough), so every reader renders any step the same way.

A mockup shows real content: the repo's fixture data, the product's own words, and the spec's names for things, never placeholder text.

A drawn mockup is HTML, with no rendered picture kept beside it: a picture would be a second copy of the page that drifts from it and takes no comment on a part. A reader that needs a picture renders the frame. A prototype's only pictures are its round videos, recorded anew each round and kept out of Git (round video).

The running prototype

A screen the project owns has its prototype as its mockup: the project's own frontend code in its repo on the lane branch, never redrawn and never a picture, either a route of its app built from its shipped components and chrome, imported or copied, or a harness page for a part the app shows on no route of its own, such as a part shown inside another product's page, or a state no route reaches safely. Each new surface sits on the real route it belongs to, new parts are built in the same design system, and the route shows real data where reading it is safe, else the repo's fixture data.

A harness page is a route the prototype serves that renders the project's own components, imported from the code the product ships, with the repo's fixture data, inside the chrome they ship in: the project's own chrome imported, and chrome it does not own drawn from that chrome's own tokens. Spec Chat knows it only as a route its mockup file names.

The seam a project offers is one prototype command, named in its repo's AGENTS.md: run from the lane worktree, it serves the app's routes and its harness pages at the root of a loopback address, as the product serves them when it ships, reading the repo's fixture data. The lane names that address with --prototype (lane command); the project adds nothing else for review.

The screen's mockup file names its route in <meta name="mockup-prototype">, a path on the running prototype, and holds all Spec Chat needs to review it, so the product's code carries nothing of Spec Chat's for the build to strip: its regions, its walkthrough's steps with the actions that reach each, and its questions; it draws no frame and carries no style.

Its regions list, <ul data-mockup-regions>, names each region a reviewer may comment on: an item with its data-anchor, unique in the mockup and stable across rounds, the selector that finds it on the route in data-select, the spec anchors it illustrates in data-illustrates, and its name in words as its text.

The running prototype answers at its root at the address its spec row names (lane command); the lane keeps it running there from the lane branch while review lasts.

The mockup page frames the route at the lane's prototype origin, where the review service serves the lane's prototype at its root (prototype proxy), so any product runs as it ships and adds nothing for review. Framed by its mockup page from that other origin, the route is held (held): every element in it is a comment target, its regions found by their selectors, and the page and the route speak only by the shell messages (prototype messages).

Each step names the actions that reach it from the step before, as the items of a list inside it: data-press with a selector presses what it finds, and data-type with a selector and data-text types that text into it. Spec Chat reaches a step by loading the route and doing every action up to it, each after the motion before it ends, so the prototype plays with its own motion and no step needs code of its own; a recorder does the same. An action whose page leaves only after a later answer, such as a fetch and then a new address, is done once its motion ends, so the next action may reach the page it leaves; a step that needs such an action has its route reach it on load. A reviewer pressing through the prototype by the action that reaches the next step moves the step bar to it; any other action leaves the step bar on no step until the reviewer picks one.

A comment on a prototype is kept in its mockup's spool, carries the step showing, and resolves by its region's selector, its target, and its quote on the route as it now runs, as a comment with no version does (marks).

A new lane branch commit while a prototype mockup page is open shows Prototype updated by agent with a Reload button, as an edit to a spec does.

With no prototype address on its row, or a prototype that does not answer, the mockup page reads Prototype not running with the route, and its conversation still holds its threads and questions.

At kickoff the build keeps the prototype's components and finishes them on the same routes, so the mockup and the build cannot drift; a screen the build must change from its accepted prototype goes back to review first.

Only chrome the project does not own is drawn: a screen of another product's own chrome, such as a host product's page a walkthrough passes through, is a drawn mockup, a self-contained page drawing its own frames from that chrome's own tokens and naming that product in <meta name="mockup-chrome"> (drawn mockup, frames). In Spec Chat, its own review chrome is a running prototype too: the lane runs its own review service from the lane branch on the repo's fixture data as its prototype, and a mockup page frames that service's pages, the review index among them, at the lane's prototype origin, each running from the runtime's own code, which holds it (prototype proxy).

Production fidelity

A mockup shows its screen as the product will ship it, never as a wireframe: a reviewer accepts what users will see (rule). A prototype is at production fidelity by construction, being the project's own components and chrome; a drawn mockup draws another product's chrome from that chrome's design source.

A drawn mockup is built from the design source of the chrome it draws, kept in the repo: the files that hold that chrome's tokens, type, components, and motion rules, such as a copy of its owner's published design tokens. Its fidelity note, <meta name="mockup-design-source">, names each such file by its path in the repo, then @ and the commit it was read at, separated by spaces. A repo with no design source has its mockups' notes read none.

Reviewing a mockup

A registered spec's mockup set is served and reviewed with it, under its row, with no registration of its own: each mockup page sits at the spec's served folder plus its path in the set. It is reviewed as the spec page is: comment mode, pins, threads, replies, edits, resolve, the one hand-off control on desktop and phone, the live read, the offline outbox, and marks that follow agent edits (review state).

Each mockup's review state is a spool beside it, <mockup>.review/, of the spec spool's shape, its versions included. A hand-off there wakes the spec row's owner by the wake rule, and the owner's scan of the lane's specs covers it, naming the mockup.

In comment mode every element inside a frame is a comment target, an image, a button, and a card among them; clicking an empty spot inside an anchored region marks that region, and selected text marks a quote, as on a spec page.

A mockup page shows the spec page's top line (top line) with a link to its spec after the back link, then Previous and Next around the screen's place in its set (next screen), and, while the mockup changed since the base, Before and After; the step bar under it for a mockup with a walkthrough; the toolbar's Comment; and the review panel, its conversation (threads), with its one hand-off control. Its Hand off never becomes Accept spec; it shows Next TBD only while one of its questions is open, and no Jev or evidence control. Its chrome is the spec page's chrome; its frames keep the mockup's own look.

On a page wide enough for both, the two frames sit side by side, desktop first; otherwise one above the other. A frame wider than its space is scaled down whole to fit it, so a reviewer sees and comments on the whole frame with no horizontal scrolling; each frame is labeled Desktop or Phone.

Fitting settles in one pass: sizing a frame to its content or its space, and the page to the chrome above it, never changes the size that sizing follows, so a mockup page, drawn or a prototype, puts no line in the error overlay as the window, a frame's content, or the chrome changes size (error overlay). A route whose resize handler answers later, debounced or throttled, can still grow its frame.

An agent edit to an open mockup shows Mockup updated by agent with a Reload button, as an edit to a spec does.

Opened as a plain file, a mockup is reviewed as a spec is as a plain file, with no compared range, no Before, and no chip on its spec.

Walkthrough

A mockup whose screen changes as its user acts, or moves on its own, holds a walkthrough: the states the screen goes through, in order, each a named step with a caption in the product's words.

A prototype draws each step itself (steps). In a drawn mockup, the runtime marks each frame with the steps reached so far in data-at, and the mockup's style draws each step from it, so the change from one step to the next plays with the mockup's own transitions, by the design source's motion rules, and under reduced motion as its reduced-motion rule shows it.

The step bar under the top line holds Play, then every step by number and name; the showing step is pressed, its caption beside the steps, and a step holding open questions or threads shows their count.

Pressing a step shows it in both frames. Play shows every step in order from the first, holding each until its motion ends and its caption can be read, then stops on the last and offers Play again; pressing a step or turning on comment mode stops it.

Outside comment mode, a prototype is pressed through as the product is, and the step bar follows the step it marks; in a drawn mockup, pressing a part a step names in data-step-by shows that step, so the reviewer presses through the screen as through the product; in comment mode the part is a comment target like any other.

A mockup page's address may name a step, and the page opens showing it; a See mockup chip for an anchor a step illustrates opens the page at that step (See mockup).

On a phone the step bar takes the screen's width and scrolls sideways within itself, Play first, each step a 44 px target, so the page never scrolls sideways.

A mockup with no walkthrough shows one state and no step bar.

Each round's video

Each round of a prototype mockup's review has a short captioned video recorded from the prototype as it then runs: its walkthrough's steps in order, each with its caption, at desktop width, and at phone width when the spec gives the screen a phone layout.

A round is a lane branch commit the prototype runs from; its videos are recorded anew from that commit, never edited, so a video never shows an older screen than the round it names.

The lane's recorder stores them in the mockup's spool, <mockup>.review/videos/, as <commit>-desktop.webm and <commit>-phone.webm, each with its captions beside it in .vtt, the step captions timed to the steps; Spec Chat only reads them, records no video, and names no recorder (who owns what).

The mockup page shows the newest round's video under the frames, on a phone its phone video when there is one, with its captions and its round's commit; each older round's video stays playable under Earlier rounds, newest first, so a spec-only commit, which records nothing, never marks a video stale.

A round's video takes comments at a moment, with or without a box (video moment), kept in the mockup's spool with the round they were made on.

A drawn mockup has no round video: its walkthrough plays live in its page.

Directions

A screen's first round may show its directions: two ways the screen could go, or three when one differs in kind, side by side on its mockup page, so the reviewer picks between real options in the round under review and no round is added for it. Shaping writes them only for a screen the change adds or changes, and only in its first round (shaping).

The screen's mockup file names them in its directions list (markup), one item per direction: its short name, the one line saying what it gives up, and on the agent's pick its one-line reason. The pick is the mockup itself: the file's route in a prototype mockup, its two frames in a drawn one. Each other direction is another route of the same running prototype, framed as the pick is (prototype route), or, in a drawn mockup, its own desktop and phone frames naming it, at production fidelity (production fidelity).

A mockup page whose file names directions shows them side by side in place of its two frames, each in one frame under its name and its gives-up line, the pick reading Agent's pick with its reason. A Desktop and Phone toggle above them switches every frame between the desktop and the phone viewport (viewports), starting at Desktop on a desktop and at Phone on a phone. Each frame is scaled whole to its share of the width, as the two frames are (fit); on a phone the directions stand one above the other, each within the screen's width.

Each direction but the standing pick, the reviewer's when one reads Your pick, else the agent's, offers Pick, a 44 px target on a phone. One press saves the reviewer's answer on the directions list, its answer the direction's name, as an answer button does (answer buttons); that direction then reads Your pick, the newest answer standing (supersede). The answer goes with the page's one Hand off, and the owner's scan reports it with the mockup's path, the directions list, and the direction's name, by which the owner finds its route or frames in the file.

Only the pick is reviewed in full: the step bar, Play, regions, questions, comment targets, pins, and the round video are the pick's (round video). Each other direction shows its route or its frames as they load; Pick is its one action, and a comment about it goes on the pick.

Directions never hold up acceptance: with no Pick pressed, Accept spec accepts the agent's pick; a pressed Pick opens a thread as any answer does, so Accept spec waits until the owner settles it (accept).

The round after brings the screen back to one direction: the one the reviewer picked, else the agent's pick, with the other directions and the directions list removed, so only a first round shows them and the page shows its two frames again. A reader of accepted mockups reads the pick alone: the file's route, or the mockup's two frames that name no direction (readers); a direction is never accepted.

Threads at a step

A comment saved on a mockup page with a walkthrough carries the step its frames showed (review state).

Its pin shows on its part only while that step shows; its chip names the part and the step's number and name, and pressing it moves both frames to that step, its part in view and ringed (chip).

A thread with no step, or on a mockup with no walkthrough, shows at every step; one whose step the mockup no longer holds shows at every step, its chip reading Step removed and the step's old name.

Before and After keep the showing step when the other version holds it, else show its first step.

Questions on the mockup

Shaping asks a question about how a screen looks or behaves on its mockup, at the part and step it is about, not in the spec's text (in-spec grilling).

Each question is an anchored TBD block in the page's questions, outside the frames, with its answer buttons, naming the parts it is about and the step it is asked at (markup); it is open as a spec's TBD is (TBD later).

The page shows each open question as the agent's bubble in its conversation (questions), and, while its step shows, a question mark on each part it is about; pressing the mark opens the question focused.

Next TBD steps through the open questions in page order: it moves both frames to the question's step, brings its part into view ringed, and opens the question focused, on a phone as a sheet under the part.

An answer is an answer comment (answer buttons), by a button or in words through Reply, the newest standing (supersede), and goes with the page's one Hand off; the owner's scan reports it with the mockup's path, the question, its parts, and its step.

The agent settles a question by editing the mockup, the spec, or both, removing the question, and replying on its thread, so the next round shows the change at the part and step the thread names.

See mockup

On a spec page served over HTTP, each anchored block that a part of a mockup in its set illustrates shows a See mockup chip after its text, one per mockup, naming that mockup's screen. Who owns what

Spec Chat owns a screen's review: its mockup file, the mockup page that frames the running prototype, the held script on the prototype's routes and the step contract, its walkthrough, the questions and threads on them, the round videos' place and their showing, and comments on a moment of any video a reviewed page shows (video moment).

The product owns the prototype: its code on the lane branch, from its shipped components, which the build keeps, its harness pages, and the prototype command that serves them (seam).

Shaping a screen on a prototype, writing the prototype and running it for the lane, recording each round’s videos, and starting the build from its components are the delivery workflow’s, outside Spec Chat; Spec Chat reviews the prototype at the address the lane command names and shows the videos the lane’s recorder stores (videos folder).

Video of the built product is recorded, and shown on its proof pages, outside Spec Chat; a page that shows it and loads the comment layer takes moment comments on it in one conversation, as any reviewed video does (video moment).

A QA check comparing a built screen with its accepted mockup reads the mockup's route or frames and its steps at the accepted commit and drives each step (readers); Spec Chat names no reader and records no video.

Nothing in this flow is built twice: one review state, one conversation, one hand-off, and one wake serve shaping, review, and the comments on proof.

Why this way

A mockup is reviewed by the spec page's own review, extended to the pages of its set, not by the comment layer: the layer comments only on elements a host anchors and keeps threads with no version, while a mockup needs comments on any part, marks that follow agent edits, and Before from Git, all of which spec page review does. No second review system, registry row, or acceptance is added.

A screen the project owns is rendered by the project's own code, never redrawn and never captured: it is at production fidelity by construction, what review accepts is what the build keeps, and the two cannot drift; a drawing or a picture is a second copy that nothing refreshes when the product changes.

Each lane's prototype runs at an origin of its own rather than under a path of the review service's: a product serves at its root as it ships, so it runs unchanged, and it shares no storage with the review pages or with another lane's prototype.

A drawn mockup whose design source changed since it was read fails rather than warns: the drawing no longer matches its chrome, and a warning passed is a drift kept.

Each round's video is recorded anew from the prototype, never kept as a picture in Git: regenerated every round, it never shows an older screen than the round it names, and it plays anywhere, on a phone or outside Spec Chat.

Directions reuse what review already has: another route of the same prototype, the mockup page's frames, one toggle, and an answer, so the reviewer chooses in the round already under review and nothing new is stored or registered; only the pick is walked through and recorded, so a direction costs one route and one line.

A question about a screen lives on its mockup, at its part and step, so the question, the place it is about, and the answer stay together and a short answer is never read out of its question.

The validator checks only what a mockup's markup shows: the route, a drawn mockup's chrome and note, tokens, fonts, focus and disabled styles, and reduced motion, and the walkthrough's names; a prototype's code is the product's and its own checks'. Whether the chrome is the product's, a drawn mockup's chrome is truly another product's, one action leads, or a screen needs a walkthrough is the reviewer's judgment, as a criterion's meaning is.
